Most screen time blockers share a fatal flaw: you can turn them off yourself. The moment willpower runs dry, one tap on a settings screen undoes everything. Tunnelvision solves this by removing the bypass entirely — your unlock button lives in someone else's pocket. This page explains exactly how that loop works and why it's effective.

## Why other blockers fail

Apps that let you disable your own blocks are really just timers with a snooze button. When the urge to scroll hits hardest, the path of least resistance wins. Tunnelvision changes the game by introducing **social accountability**: to get back into Instagram or TikTok, you have to show a real person that you've earned it.

## The accountability loop

<Steps>
  <Step title="You hit your daily screen time limit">
    You set a limit — say, 30 minutes of social media per day. Once you reach it, Tunnelvision locks every app and website on your block list. There is no secret settings menu to tap through. The block holds.
  </Step>

  <Step title="You complete your habits">
    Instead of looking for a workaround, you shift your attention to your habits. Log a workout, finish a reading session, or complete a study block. Each habit you mark done becomes a piece of evidence attached to your unlock request.
  </Step>

  <Step title="You send an unlock request to your friend">
    When you're ready, tap **Request Unlock**, choose how many minutes of screen time you want, and send the request. Your friend receives a notification that includes every habit you completed today alongside your recent streak — a transparent, honest picture of your effort.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Your friend reviews and decides">
    Your accountability partner sees your completed habits, how long your current streak is, and how consistent you've been over time. Armed with that context, they make a real judgement call. No rubber-stamping — they can approve, deny, or simply ignore the request.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Approved: you get a timed window of access">
    If your friend taps **Approve**, your blocked apps unlock for exactly the number of minutes you requested. When that window closes, the block re-engages automatically. You don't need to do anything — it resets on its own.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Denied or ignored: you stay locked out">
    If your friend denies the request or doesn't respond, the apps stay locked. This is the core of the system — the friction is real, the consequence is real, and the decision belongs to someone who isn't driven by your momentary craving.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What your friend sees

Your accountability partner doesn't take your word for it. When they open a pending unlock request, they see:

- **Today's completed habits** — every habit you've logged for the day, with timestamps
- **Your current streak** — how many consecutive days you've hit your habits
- **Streak history** — a longer view of your consistency over time

This gives your friend the full picture before they decide. A week-long streak of completed workouts tells a very different story than two habits logged in the last hour after a week of nothing.

## Focus Schedules

Beyond daily limits, Tunnelvision lets you set **Focus Schedules** — recurring blocks tied to specific times of day. Lock distracting apps every weekday morning, during study hours, or after a set bedtime. Schedules engage and disengage automatically, so you build structure without having to think about it each day.

<Note>
  Tunnelvision's blocks are enforced using **Apple's Screen Time framework**, which operates at the iOS system level. This means blocks cannot be bypassed by deleting the Tunnelvision app or adjusting settings inside it. The restrictions are applied by the operating system itself, making them significantly harder to circumvent than app-level solutions.
</Note>

## Privacy

Your usage data stays on your device. Tunnelvision does not sell your habits, screen time, or behaviour to third parties. The only person who sees your habit completions and streak is the friend you explicitly invite as your accountability partner.
