As an accountability partner in Tunnelvision, you hold the unlock button for a friend who is trying to limit their screen time. When they hit their daily limit and complete their habits, they send a request to you — and you decide whether to approve more time or keep them locked out. It's a simple role, but a meaningful one.

<Note>
  You do NOT need to be on the same Tunnelvision plan as the person you're supporting. You can be an accountability partner on a free account regardless of what plan your friend uses.
</Note>

## Getting Started

<Steps>
  <Step title="Download Tunnelvision">
    Install Tunnelvision on your device from the [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tunnelvision-habit-tracker/id6752546483). You need the app installed to receive and respond to unlock requests.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Create your account">
    Open the app and complete the sign-up flow to create your Tunnelvision account.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Accept the friend invitation">
    The person you're supporting will send you a friend invitation from within the app. Open Tunnelvision, navigate to the **Friends** tab, and accept their invitation. Once connected, you'll be their designated accountability partner.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Turn on notifications">
    Enable push notifications for Tunnelvision so you're alerted the moment your friend sends an unlock request. Without notifications, you may miss requests and leave them waiting. You can enable notifications in your device's **Settings → Tunnelvision → Notifications**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What You See When a Request Comes In

When your friend hits their daily screen time limit and requests an unlock, you'll receive a notification. Inside the app, each request shows you:

- **Your friend's name** — so you know who is asking
- **Habits completed today** — the specific tasks they logged as proof of effort
- **Their current streak** — how many consecutive days they've completed their habits

This context is there for a reason: use it. A long streak and a full set of completed habits is a strong signal. A request with nothing logged is a signal too.

## How to Approve a Request

Tap **Approve** to grant your friend access. The block will lift and your friend gets the break they earned.

## How to Deny a Request

Tap **Deny** to keep your friend locked out. They remain blocked until they send another request. Denying isn't harsh — it's the job you signed up for.

## Tips for Being a Good Accountability Partner

<Accordion title="Don't auto-approve">
  The whole point of having a human in the loop is that a human actually looks at the request. If you approve every request instantly without checking the habits, you're not an accountability partner — you're a rubber stamp. Take three seconds to look at what they logged.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Actually look at the habits">
  Your friend's habit completions are the proof of effort. If they logged a workout, a reading session, or a study block, that context matters. If the habit list is empty, that context matters too. Make your decision based on what you see.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Be consistent">
  Unpredictable responses undermine the system. If you approve generously some days and strictly other days with no clear pattern, your friend can't calibrate their behavior. Decide on your standard — and stick to it.
</Accordion>
