Tunnelvision blocks distracting apps at the operating system level using Apple's Screen Time API. Unlike browser extensions or soft timers you can dismiss with a single tap, these blocks are enforced by iOS itself — once your time is up, the app is locked and only your designated friend can open it back up.

## How Hard Blocks Work

Tunnelvision integrates directly with Apple's Screen Time framework, the same system that powers built-in iOS parental controls. When you set a block, iOS enforces it — not just Tunnelvision. That means:

- **No override button.** You cannot tap "Ignore Limit" and continue scrolling.
- **No app deletion workaround.** Reinstalling a blocked app does not reset the block.
- **No Settings escape hatch.** Disabling Screen Time requires your friend's approval PIN, which you don't have.

## Supported Apps

Tunnelvision can block any app installed on your device. It comes pre-configured to target the most common sources of distraction:

- Instagram
- TikTok
- YouTube
- X (Twitter)
- Snapchat

You can also block any other app or entire app categories (Games, Entertainment, Social Networking, and more) directly from the Tunnelvision app selector.

## Setting a Daily Time Limit

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Tunnelvision and tap Add Block">
    From the home screen, tap the **+** button to create a new block rule.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose your apps">
    Select individual apps or pick an entire category. You can mix and match — block Instagram and Games together under one rule.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set your daily limit">
    Choose how many minutes per day you want to allow. When the clock hits zero, the block activates automatically.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save the rule">
    Tap **Save**. Tunnelvision writes the rule to Screen Time immediately. The limit resets every night at midnight.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Website Blocking

You can block specific websites alongside apps. Add any URL — `instagram.com`, `youtube.com`, `reddit.com` — and Tunnelvision will block access in Safari and all other browsers on your device when the limit is active.

Website blocks follow the same OS-level enforcement as app blocks. Opening a blocked site in any browser shows the same locked screen.

## What Happens When You Hit the Limit

The moment your daily allowance runs out, every blocked app closes to a locked screen. You'll see a Tunnelvision overlay rather than the standard iOS "Time Limit" prompt. That screen tells you:

- Which habits you've completed today
- How much extra time you can request
- The button to send an unlock request to your friend

Your friend then decides whether to approve. Until they do, the app stays locked. Learn more about how that approval flow works in [Friend Unlocks](/features/friend-unlocks).

## Hard Blocks vs. Soft Blocks

Most focus apps use **soft blocks** — a reminder screen with a button that says something like "One More Minute" or "Ignore for Today." You are one tap away from bypassing them entirely.

Tunnelvision uses **hard blocks**. There is no ignore button. There is no override. The only path back into a blocked app is through your friend. That friction is the point.

<Note>
  App blocking requires **iOS 18.4 or later** (iPad 18.4+ and macOS 15.4+ on Apple M1 or later are also supported). You must also grant Tunnelvision Screen Time permissions during onboarding — without them, OS-level enforcement is not possible. If you skipped that step, go to **Settings → Screen Time** and authorize Tunnelvision to manage restrictions.
</Note>
